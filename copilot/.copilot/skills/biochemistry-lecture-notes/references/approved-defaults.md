# Approved Teaching and Writing Defaults

These choices were explicitly approved while producing the first unit of
`26BIH-101 Chemistry of Biomolecules`.

## Preferred Input Package

- Official syllabus in DOCX, PDF, or another structured format, including
  outcomes and contact hours.
- A searchable or OCR-enabled local PDF of the legally accessed prescribed
  text. Large local PDFs are acceptable.
- Exact public URLs rather than only the name of a website.
- Existing draft notes as material to audit and rebuild, not as text that must
  be preserved.
- A previously approved unit package as the style and quality exemplar.

## Scope and Audience

- Build the entire course one unit at a time.
- Default audience: first-semester B.Tech Biotechnology students at an Indian
  university.
- Assume a mixed and uneven 10+2 chemistry background.
- Do not create a broad prerequisite chemistry primer. Define only the
  prerequisite idea needed at the point where it is used.
- If the official syllabus identifies Master's students or another level,
  adjust conceptual depth automatically while retaining the same quality and
  document conventions.

## Purpose and Depth

- Primary purpose: self-contained post-lecture study notes that also support
  live teaching.
- Write at a foundational first-year level with selective textbook depth.
- Keep the core strictly aligned to the official syllabus.
- Add only the supporting context necessary to make the explanation complete.
- Focus mainly on fundamental biochemistry rather than applications.
- Do not include laboratory experiments unless the user asks for them.
- Include equations where useful, but no worked numerical problems by default.
- Show representative chemical structures rather than exhaustive structural
  catalogues.

## Unit Size and Organisation

- Produce one student-notes document per unit.
- Divide a unit into two or three coherent parts according to its size.
- A five-contact-hour unit means five 50-minute lectures.
- Approved five-hour baseline: roughly 7,000–8,500 words and 18–25 A4 pages,
  including figures and tables.
- Scale density sensibly for larger units instead of forcing every unit into the
  same page count.
- Preserve one unit document even when it has multiple internal parts, unless
  the user explicitly requests separate volumes.

## Narrative Style

- Use clear textbook-style prose with no guiding questions.
- The writing must feel like natural faculty-prepared material, not generated
  text.
- Avoid repetitive templates such as constant "Why this matters", "Key
  takeaway", or question-and-answer boxes.
- Vary paragraph and sentence length naturally.
- Use approximately balanced prose, bullets, and tables.
- Use bullets and tables only when they genuinely improve explanation or
  comparison.
- Use Indian/British English: organisation, stabilise, sulphur, haemoglobin.
- Use current IUPAC/IUBMB terminology. If a prescribed textbook uses an older
  term, put it in parentheses when useful.
- Bold each important term at first use, define it in context, and expand each
  abbreviation once.

## Recurring Student-Notes Structure

1. Brief unit introduction
2. Learning outcomes without CO or Bloom tags
3. Detailed narrative divided into two or three parts
4. Tables, equations, and representative figures where pedagogically useful
5. At most one brief historical timeline when it adds context
6. Concise unit summary
7. Ten two-mark subjective questions with CO and Bloom tags
8. Numbered references and suggested reading

CO and Bloom tags should appear on assessment items, not throughout the
teaching narrative.

## Figures and Visuals

- Keep the visual count restrained: normally 3–6 figures per unit.
- Prefer original simplified schematics and openly licensed images.
- A figure must clarify one idea and remain understandable to both teacher and
  first-year student.
- Do not include visually impressive but overly specialised textbook diagrams.
- Use figure captions and cite every external figure.
- Do not use colour as the only way to communicate meaning.
- Optimise for screen reading and ordinary colour or greyscale printing.

## Document Template

- Primary editable format: polished Microsoft Word DOCX.
- No fixed university template or branding is assumed.
- Use a clean academic design with one restrained accent palette.
- Use readable typography and contrast without adding formal accessibility
  metadata unless requested.

## Citations

- Use numbered citations for figures, quotations, numerical data, historical
  claims, definitions, and nonstandard scientific statements.
- Avoid cluttering every ordinary explanatory paragraph with citations.
- End each unit with a numbered bibliography.
- Preferred authoritative supplements include IUPAC/IUBMB, NCBI Bookshelf,
  RCSB PDB-101, OpenStax, reputable universities, and peer-reviewed/open-access
  sources.
